Healthy Ageing in Australia

About one in six Australians (17.5%) is aged 65 or over in 2025, up from 8.7% in 1975. We are living longer, but are those extra years healthy ones and is care in the right places?

This visualisation is for Australians planning for later life and the families who support them. It shows where older Australians live, how healthy they are, what helps them age well, and whether care is available where they need it.

01 · Population

A nation growing older.

The shape of our population is changing, and the change looks different depending on where you live.

Australia's population aged between 1975 and 2025. Darker bars highlight people aged 65 and over.

Bars show five-year age groups as a share of the total population.

Coastal and regional areas have larger shares of older residents than city fringes.

Tasmania and South Australia lead in ageing. The Northern Territory remains youngest, with fewer than 10% aged 65 and over.

More people reach older age—but how healthy are those extra years?

02 · Health and wellbeing

Longer lives. More healthy years?

Life expectancy tells us how long we may live. Health conditions show how well.

Longer lives often include chronic illness. Who has the best chance of ageing well?

03 · Ageing well

What helps, and who misses out.

Staying active supports healthy ageing, but activity and good health are not shared equally.

Inactivity rises with age: 28% of people aged 75 and over did no activity last week, compared with 6% of 18–24-year-olds.

Only 48% of people aged 75 and over met the 150-minute weekly activity guideline.

Columns total 100%. Hover for percentages and population counts.

Among people aged 65 and over, 35% in the most disadvantaged areas have 3 or more chronic conditions, compared with 21% in the least disadvantaged.

The rate for 45-64-year-olds in the most disadvantaged areas is already 25%.

Disadvantage compares the highest and lowest fifths of areas using the Australian Bureau of Statistics Index of Relative Socio-economic Disadvantage.

Among people aged 75 and over, 36% rate their health excellent or very good; 31% rate it fair or poor.

Self-rated health reflects people's own view of their wellbeing.

04 · Access to care

Is care where it is needed?

First where the need is growing, then where the care is, then whether the two match.

Australia's population aged 85 and over grew from about 263,000 in 2001 to over 600,000 in 2025.

Regions with at least 2.5% aged 85 and over increased from 11 to 146.

Grey indicates no estimate for that year.

Australia's 2,772 aged care homes and Multi-Purpose Services cluster in cities and along the east coast.

The 182 Multi-Purpose Services combine hospital and aged care in remote areas.

Residential aged care use averages 42 people per 1,000 people aged 65 and over: about 17 in Bribie-Beachmere and 109 in Unley. Older populations do not always mean higher care use.

Rate: residential aged care users (Australian Institute of Health and Welfare) per 1,000 residents aged 65 and over (Australian Bureau of Statistics) in each region (Statistical Area Level 3).

More years, better years? Partly.

  • Australians live longer, but chronic illness remains common.
  • Disadvantaged communities face poorer health.
  • Care does not always match local need.

Healthier ageing needs active lifestyles, greater equity and care where it is needed.
